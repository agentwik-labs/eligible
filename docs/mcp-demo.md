**Question:** Can I use my FSA for sunscreen and a massage?

  - result: verdict **dual_purpose**: Sunscreen is covered only when bought mainly for a medical reason, such as preventing or treating a skin condition. It is also an ordinary personal-care product.

  cites: elist:sunscreen, p502:what-are-medical-expenses, p502:personal-use-items
  - result: verdict **needs_prescription**: Massage is covered only as medical treatment for a condition, with a physician's prescription or letter of medical necessity. It isn't covered for general well-being.

  cites: elist:massage-therapy, p502:therapy, p502:health-club-dues

## Claude's answer

Maybe, but neither is automatic. Both depend on why you're buying them.

**Sunscreen (SPF 50): dual-purpose**
- It counts only if you buy it mainly for a medical reason, such as preventing or treating a skin condition.
- Ordinary sun protection falls under personal care, and that isn't eligible.
- If you have a medical reason, like a history of skin cancer or a photosensitivity condition, get a note from your doctor and keep it with your receipt.

**Massage: needs a prescription**
- It's covered as medical treatment for a specific condition, with a physician's prescription or letter of medical necessity.
- Massage for general well-being or relaxation isn't covered.

Publication 502 says you can't include costs "to improve one's general health or to relieve physical or mental discomfort not related to a particular medical condition."

Your plan administrator has the final say, and some plans ask for documentation up front. Ask them before you spend. This is general information, not tax advice.
